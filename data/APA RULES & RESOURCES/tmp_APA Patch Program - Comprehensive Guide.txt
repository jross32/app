APA Patch Program – Comprehensive Guide
APA (American Poolplayers Association) leagues offer various  embroidered patches  to recognize player
achievements in 8-Ball and 9-Ball formats. Below we detail  all the patches currently available , which
patches are actually awarded in the Rio Grande Valley (RGV) APA  under League Operator Erin Lacy, and
how to obtain patches not handed out locally. We’ve also included images and descriptions of each patch for
clarity.
APA 8-Ball Achievement Patches
APA’s 8-Ball format has several performance-based patches for specific feats during a match:
8-on-the-Break (8OB):  Earned by legally sinking the 8-ball on the break without fouling . (This
instant win is a thrilling feat; patch designs often show an 8-ball exploding out of a rack.) For
example, the APA “8 On The Break” patch is awarded for pocketing the 8 on a legal break .
8-Ball Break and Run (8BR):  Earned by making at least one ball on the break and then running the
table, pocketing all your balls plus the 8 without giving up a turn . This is essentially a perfect
game from the break in 8-ball.
8-Ball Mini-Slam : Awarded for accomplishing both  an 8-on-the-break and an 8-break-and-run in the
same night  (single match or league night) . Some leagues issue a dedicated “8-Ball Mini Slam”
patch instead of two separate patches . (If separate patches are given, a player might receive
both an 8OB and 8BR patch; some LOs, however , opt to give one combined Mini-Slam patch to limit
the number of patches.)
Rackless Night:  Awarded in 8-ball when your opponent never gets to break  during the match .
In APA’s alternating-break format, a “rackless” match means you won the lag and then won every
rack you broke, so the other player never broke. (This indicates a dominant performance.) The patch
often features a broken rack or “no rack” symbol. Per APA rules, a Rackless patch is earned by “starting
out breaking in a match and continuing to win (thus you keep the break) until the match is won,” so your
opponent doesn’t rack at all . Note:  Some areas restrict Rackless patches to lower-skilled players or
require the opponent to be of equal/higher skill , but generally in RGV APA any player can earn it
with the proper scenario.
Example APA patch for an “8 on the Break,” awarded when a player sinks the 8-ball on the break in 8-Ball .
Many APA 8OB patches feature an 8-ball graphic and bold text celebrating the achievement.
“I Beat a 6” / “I Beat a 7”:  These are upset  patches in 8-ball, awarded when a lower-skilled player
defeats a high skill level. In APA 8-ball, skill levels range 2–7, so beating a Skill Level 6 or 7 as an
underdog is notable. For instance,  an “I Beat a 7” patch is earned by winning against an opponent
ranked SL7 in 8-ball  (usually the winner must be at least 2 levels lower – e.g. SL5 or below – as• 1

patches are available upon request if not handed out immediately).
APA 9-Ball Achievement Patches
The 9-Ball format has its own set of patches, parallel to 8-ball in many ways:
9-on-the-Snap (9OS or 9OB):  Earned by pocketing the 9-ball on a legal break without fouling .
This is the 9-ball equivalent of the 8-on-the-break. (Many patches actually say “9 on the Snap.”) Any
player who breaks and sinks the 9 earns this patch .
9-Ball Break and Run (9BR):  Earned by legally making at least one ball on the break and running all
remaining balls in the rack, ending by sinking the 9, without giving up the turn . This is a perfect
game in 9-ball. All skill levels are eligible for 9B&R patches . 
Example APA patch for a “9-Ball Break & Run.” This patch is earned by breaking and running the entire rack in 9-
ball . The design usually features the 9-ball; completing a 9B&R is a mark of excellent play.
9-Ball Mini-Slam : Awarded for scoring a 9-on-the-snap and a 9-break-and-run in the same night .
Like the 8-ball mini-slam, some LOs (including RGV APA) issue a single “9-Ball Mini Slam” patch when
both feats occur on the same league night, instead of two separate patches. (If RGV APA players
mark both achievements, Erin will likely issue the Mini-Slam patch to cover both, rather than two
patches, as a policy to manage patch inventory.)
20-0 Shutout:  Earned by winning a 9-ball match with a perfect score of  20–0 . In APA 9-ball, a
player races to a certain point total (14, 19, or 21 points depending on skill level). A “20-0” means you
held your opponent to zero points while reaching 20 or more yourself – a dominant shutout. Often
called the “Skunk”  patch (and some older designs even feature a skunk), it signifies a blowout victory
. Any 9-ball player who wins 20-0 against an established opponent earns this patch .RGV APA:9

“I Beat a 7/8/9” (9-Ball Upset patches):  In 9-ball, high skill levels go up to SL9 for males (SL7 is
highest for females in some areas). APA offers upset patches for beating skill level 7, 8, or 9 in 9-ball
. Typically, these are for lower-skilled players (SL5 or below) who defeat an SL7/8/9 in a regular
match. For example, the “I Beat a 9” patch is earned by beating a SL9 in 9-ball during regular league play
or higher-level tournaments .RGV APA:  Historically, Erin’s league has not been handing out these
“I beat ___” upset patches for 9-ball, similar to 8-ball. The majority of patches distributed in RGV are
performance-based, not opponent-based. So, while the patches exist (and some players want  them),
you would not automatically get an “I beat a 9” patch in RGV APA unless policies change or you
request/buy one yourself .
Team & Session Patches (All Formats)
Some patches recognize team achievements or multi-format accomplishments:
Clean Sweep (5-0) Patch:  Awarded when your team wins all 5 matches  in a league night (applies to
both 8-ball and 9-ball team matches) . Every player who played in those wins gets a patch. (For 9-
ball, although points decide the team win, APA still uses a “5-0” sweep patch if your team won all five
individual matchups.) Forfeits don’t count – you must actually win five played matches for a sweep .
The patch often says “5-0” or “Clean Sweep” with a graphic (e.g. a broom). RGV APA:  Yes, RGV does
award Clean Sweep patches for both formats. Make sure to mark “5-0 sweep” on the scoresheet if
your team blanks the opponents, and Erin will include the patch in your packet.
An APA “Clean Sweep” patch (5-0 patch) for team shutouts . This patch is given to each team member who
played in a 5–0 victory. Designs often feature a broom or “5-0” text, indicating a complete sweep.
Grand Slam Patch:  Highly coveted, this is earned by accomplishing  all four  major feats in one
session: an 8-on-the-break, an 8-break-and-run, a 9-on-the-snap, and a 9-break-and-run . Earning
a Grand Slam means you’ve achieved both the 8-Ball and 9-Ball Mini Slams in one session – a sign of
all-around excellence. APA describes the Grand Slam patch as “a great accomplishment” awarded when a
player gets at least one of each patch (8OB, 8BR, 9OB, 9BR) in the same session .RGV APA:  Yes, if you
manage this, Erin Lacy will recognize it! Grand Slam patches are typically free  and given at session’s
end once verified . (You might not see it until the session is over , so ensure all your individual
achievements were noted so the LO knows you qualified.) Not many achieve this, but RGV has
awarded it to the rare players who do.
MVP or Top Gun Patches:  Many APA areas run MVP/Top Gun programs where the top player(s) in
each division or tier each session get recognized. In some areas, the prize includes an MVP patch.
For example, First Coast APA gives an MVP patch plus a prize to the highest finishing players in each
tier . RGV APA:  RGV does hold MVP tournaments and honors top performers, but Erin typically
awards trophies or invites to MVP events rather than a patch alone. Check with Erin if RGV has a
specific MVP patch – as of now, RGV’s focus is on the tournaments (with trophies/prizes) more so
than issuing a patch. •

Higher-Level Tournament Patches:  APA National/Regional events (e.g., City/World Qualifiers) often
have special patches for accomplishments during those tournaments. For instance, some areas give
“HLT 8-on-Break” or “HLT Break&Run” patches if you do it in a Tri-Cup or World Qualifier (marked
with “HLT”). Birmingham APA notes High Level Tournament patches separately . RGV APA:  When
RGV players go to regionals or Vegas, they may receive special event patches from those events (for
example, national singles champions get patches, etc.). Locally, RGV doesn’t have separate designs
for HLT – they use the same patch designs, but the achievement is usually just noted verbally or via
certificates. 
Milestone & Leadership Patches
APA also awards patches for longevity and leadership roles:
Matches Played (Milestone) Patches:  These commemorate the total number of lifetime matches
you’ve played in APA (8-ball + 9-ball combined). APA produces patches for milestones like 100, 250,
500, 750, 1000  (and some areas even go beyond to 1500, 2000, etc.) . For example, once you
reach 100 matches, you can get the 100 Matches patch; higher milestones like 500 and 1000 have their
own patches . RGV APA:  Erin Lacy’s policy is that milestone patches are available upon request .
They are not automatically given the week you hit the number , but if you or your captain lets her
know (or marks the scoresheet), she will provide the patch. RGV definitely has 100, 250, 500, 750,
1000 in stock for those who qualify – and if you surpass 1000, coordinate with Erin for higher-level
patches (APA does have 1500+ as needed). Always feel free to ask for your milestone patch if you hit
the magic number and didn’t get it yet.
Team Captain / Co-Captain Patch:  APA appreciates team leaders by offering patches for serving as
a captain or co-captain for a full session . These typically say “Team Captain” or “Team Co-
Captain.” RGV APA:  These leadership patches are not automatically handed out  every session in RGV.
However , Erin does have them available. In some cases, RGV gives captains a small gift at session’s
end (like a pin or token) instead of a patch. If you specifically want the patch, you can request it –
“Team Captain” and “Co-Captain” patches are one per player per year upon request  in some areas .
So, RGV captains can certainly get one (especially if you haven’t before) by asking Erin or marking the
patch request form.
Division Representative Patch:  Awarded if you serve as a Division Rep (a player-volunteer who
helps the LO in a division) . RGV APA:  These are rare and given only to those appointed as reps. If
RGV has division reps, Erin will provide this patch as appropriate.
Sportsmanship Award Patch:  APA encourages good sportsmanship by letting players nominate
others for a Sportsmanship patch . If someone on another team exemplifies great attitude,
you can write a nomination, and the league may award them a “Good Sportsmanship” patch. It
usually shows a handshake or trophy icon. RGV APA:  Erin Lacy does support sportsmanship awards.
While not common every week, if you send a nomination (RGV has a form for Good Sportsmanship
nominations),  the  league  office  will  review  it.  Worthy  recipients  will  indeed  get  a  patch  saying
“Sportsmanship” as a special recognition . So yes, RGV APA has this patch – but it’s only awarded
when you nominate someone who truly earned it.•

Patches RGV APA Does  and Does Not  Award
To summarize for RGV (Erin Lacy’s league):
Awarded in RGV:  All the standard  performance  patches are given: 8-on-Break, 8-Break&Run, 9-on-
Snap, 9-Break&Run, Rackless, 20-0 Shutout, Clean Sweep (team 5-0), and Grand Slam (if earned)
. RGV also honors  Mini-Slams  in both formats – but instead of two separate patches, you’ll
receive  a  single  Mini-Slam  patch  per  format  (one  for  8-ball  night,  one  for  9-ball  night)  if  you
accomplish both feats in one night. First Match win patches are available for new players, and
milestone patches (100+ matches) are given on request. Sportsmanship patches are awarded if
nominations are made (not automatic). Team Captain/Co-Captain patches can be obtained (usually
on request at end of session, since not automatically distributed). Essentially,  RGV APA provides
patches for game feats and team feats, and will provide other patches if you ask or qualify ,
even if they’re not routinely handed out.
Not Typically Awarded in RGV:  The “I Beat a [Skill]” upset patches have  not been part of RGV’s
regular patch program. As confirmed by player discussions, some LOs choose not to offer these
(often due to cost or to discourage any unsportsmanlike gloating) . Erin Lacy historically did not
include “I Beat a 7/8/9” patches in weekly awards. Also, the “Beat the LO” patch is essentially moot in
RGV due to Erin focusing on league management (she isn’t playing weekly league matches against
members). If Erin ever does play and you beat her , well, you could lobby for that patch – but in
general it’s not in circulation. 
Additionally, patches like MVP/Top Gun  are not given as patches in RGV – instead, MVPs get invitations to
the MVP tournament and sometimes trophies or other prizes, but not a specific patch each session. Lastly,
High Level Tournament  specific patches (like ones labeled for City or Vegas events) are not distributed locally;
any special event patches would come from the events themselves or not at all.
In summary, RGV APA awards the common patches that most players earn during league play, but
excludes a few of the rarer or “extra” patches unless requested.  This is similar to many APA areas – for
instance, one APA member noted their league operator only offers “the most common ones” and not things like
the upset patches . Erin’s approach has been to focus on core achievements (so everyone gets recognition
for great shots and matches), while keeping the patch program manageable.
Obtaining Patches Not Handed Out Locally
Just because RGV APA doesn’t normally give a certain patch doesn’t mean you can’t get it. APA players often
collect patches from various sources. Here are ways to obtain patches Erin Lacy might not automatically
provide:
Request from the League Operator:  The first step is simply asking Erin. She has access to the APA
patch inventory and can order any official patch. If you earn something like “I Beat a 7” and really
want  that  patch  for  your  collection,  talk  to  her .  Many  LOs  are  willing  to  accommodate  one-off
requests (possibly for a small fee to cover costs). RGV APA uses Patch Request Forms – just write in
the patch you want (with details of your achievement) and there’s a good chance you can get it at the•

APA Patch Store / Other League Operators:  Some APA areas sell patches directly to players online.
For example, the Maryland APA has an APA Patch Store  where players can purchase extra or less-

Trading with Players:  APA patches are often traded or given among players, especially at national
events. If you know someone from another area or go to APA Vegas events, you can sometimes
trade duplicates for ones you need. (Many veteran players have extras of common patches and

Online Marketplaces:  As a last resort, you can find APA patches on eBay and similar sites (sold by
collectors). For example, eBay frequently has listings for specific patches (like “APA 5-0 Clean Sweep
patch”  or sets of “I Beat ___” patches).  However, buying patches you didn’t earn  is a bit like
buying trophies – it’s fine for completing a collection or replacing a lost patch, but wear them with
integrity! It’s always better to earn it or get it through your LO if possible.
In short, any patch APA offers is out there and can be obtained . RGV APA focuses on the core patches,
but Erin Lacy won’t begrudge you getting the others – she just doesn’t automatically stock or distribute
every  single  type.  If  you’re  a  RGV  player  who  achieved  something  notable  that  isn’t  usually  awarded,
communicate with the league office. And if that fails, the wider APA community and marketplace can fill the
gap. (One player in an APA discussion said: “My LO doesn’t pass these out, but I want one,” regarding an I Beat a 7
patch  – a common sentiment. The solutions were to ask the LO or find it online.)
Conclusion and Last 10 Years of Patch Evolution
Over the last decade, APA’s patch program has expanded slightly (adding things like the  upset patches,
Mini-Slams,  and even higher match count patches as more players hit milestones). But the core patches
have remained the same. An APA member from 10+ years ago would recognize almost all the patches used
today: 8-on-break, 9-on-snap, break-and-runs, shutouts, rackless, and clean sweeps were all around, and
they still are . What’s changed is some leagues now offer more  patches (to reward a wider variety of
feats), whereas others keep it basic. For example, years ago some areas didn’t give “I Beat ___” patches at
all, considering them optional – and that’s still the case in RGV APA today. Conversely, new patches like “I
Won My First Match” have been introduced to encourage beginners (this wasn’t common a decade ago).
And APA has added higher-tier milestone patches (1500+ matches) as players stay in league longer .
Erin Lacy’s RGV APA has largely stuck to the classic patches to reward great shots and matches. To recap, if
you’re playing in RGV APA, you can earn the following patches through normal league play:
8-Ball:  8 on Break, 8 Break & Run, Rackless, 8-Ball Mini-Slam (or the two individual patches if policy
changes), and I Beat 6/7 (available on request, but not automatic).
9-Ball:  9 on Snap, 9 Break & Run, 20-0 Shutout, 9-Ball Mini-Slam, and I Beat 7/8/9 (on request only).36
